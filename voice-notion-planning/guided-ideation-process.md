# Guided Ideation Process

A conversational framework for expanding raw ideas into complete project descriptions through structured exploration.

## Philosophy

**The Expanding Canvas**: Ideas start as seeds and grow through conversation. Each exchange adds detail, surfaces assumptions, identifies gaps, and builds toward a complete picture. The Notion page serves as a living document that visually expands in real-time.

**Active Gap Detection**: Don't wait for the user to think of everything. Proactively identify what's missing, ask probing questions, and suggest areas to explore. The goal is a description complete enough to hand to an implementer.

**Conversational Flow**: Even though the process is structured, it should feel like a natural brainstorming conversation, not a form being filled out.

---

## The Six Phases of Ideation

### Phase 1: CAPTURE - The Spark (2-5 min)
**Goal:** Capture the raw idea without judgment or structure

**Approach:**
- Let them talk freely about the idea
- Ask "What else?" repeatedly to get all initial thoughts out
- Capture exact words - don't paraphrase yet
- Note emotional energy and enthusiasm areas

**Key Questions:**
- "Tell me about this idea..."
- "What made you think of this?"
- "What's the most exciting part to you?"
- "What else comes to mind?"

**Notion Updates:**
- Create Ideation Canvas page
- Add raw capture to "The Spark" section
- Tag initial themes detected

---

### Phase 2: CLARIFY - Understanding the Core (5-10 min)
**Goal:** Distill the essence and establish boundaries

**Approach:**
- Reflect back what you heard
- Identify the core value proposition
- Separate the idea from implementation details
- Surface unstated assumptions

**Key Questions:**
- "So the core of this is... [reflection]. Is that right?"
- "What problem does this solve?"
- "Who is this for, specifically?"
- "What would success look like?"
- "What is this NOT trying to do?"

**Mental Models to Apply:**
- **Abstraction Laddering**: Move up (why?) and down (how?) to find the right level
- **Inversion**: What would make this fail?

**Notion Updates:**
- Add "Core Concept" summary (1-2 sentences)
- Add "Problem Statement"
- Add "Target Audience"
- Add "Success Criteria"
- Add "Out of Scope" boundaries

---

### Phase 3: EXPAND - Exploring Dimensions (10-20 min)
**Goal:** Systematically explore all facets of the idea

**Approach:**
- Work through each dimension conversationally
- Use "What about...?" to open new areas
- Let tangents happen but track them
- Continuously update Notion as you go

**Dimensions to Explore:**

**1. User/Audience**
- Who exactly uses this? (personas)
- What's their current situation?
- What triggers them to need this?
- What do they currently do instead?

**2. Functionality**
- What can users do with this?
- What's the core workflow?
- What are edge cases?
- What features are must-have vs nice-to-have?

**3. Technical**
- What technologies might be involved?
- What integrations are needed?
- What are the constraints?
- What already exists that could be leveraged?

**4. Context**
- When/where is this used?
- What environment does it operate in?
- What other tools/systems does it interact with?
- What's the broader ecosystem?

**5. Business/Value**
- What's the value proposition?
- What's the effort vs impact?
- What resources are needed?
- What's the timeline thinking?

**Mental Models to Apply:**
- **Decomposition**: Break complex aspects into components
- **Constraint Relaxation**: "What if [limitation] didn't exist?"

**Notion Updates:**
- Populate each dimension section
- Add sub-bullets as details emerge
- Mark dimensions as "explored" vs "needs more"

---

### Phase 4: CHALLENGE - Stress Testing (5-10 min)
**Goal:** Find weaknesses, gaps, and assumptions before they become problems

**Approach:**
- Play devil's advocate constructively
- Ask "what if" scenarios
- Identify dependencies and risks
- Surface hidden complexity

**Key Questions:**
- "What could go wrong?"
- "What are we assuming that might not be true?"
- "What happens when [edge case]?"
- "What's the hardest part of this?"
- "What don't we know that we need to know?"

**Mental Models to Apply:**
- **Pre-Mortem**: "It's 6 months later and this failed. What happened?"
- **Adversarial Thinking**: How could this be misused or broken?
- **Five Whys**: Dig into root causes of concerns

**Notion Updates:**
- Add "Risks & Challenges" section
- Add "Open Questions" list
- Add "Assumptions to Validate"
- Flag any dimensions that need revisiting

---

### Phase 5: SYNTHESIZE - Pulling It Together (5-10 min)
**Goal:** Create a coherent, complete project description

**Approach:**
- Review all captured content
- Organize into logical structure
- Fill remaining gaps
- Create clear narrative

**Key Questions:**
- "Looking at everything, what's the one-sentence summary?"
- "What's the priority order for features?"
- "What would the MVP look like?"
- "What's the first step?"

**Notion Updates:**
- Write "Executive Summary" (2-3 paragraphs)
- Create "Feature Priority Matrix"
- Define "MVP Scope"
- Draft "Next Steps"

---

### Phase 6: VALIDATE - Final Review (2-5 min)
**Goal:** Confirm completeness and readiness

**Approach:**
- Run through completeness checklist
- Ask for final thoughts
- Identify any remaining discomfort
- Confirm next actions

**Completeness Checklist:**
- [ ] Problem is clearly defined
- [ ] Target audience is specific
- [ ] Core features are listed
- [ ] Success criteria are measurable
- [ ] Scope boundaries are clear
- [ ] Technical approach is outlined
- [ ] Risks are identified
- [ ] MVP is defined
- [ ] Next step is actionable

**Notion Updates:**
- Mark checklist items
- Add "Session Summary"
- Set "Readiness Status"
- Create linked tasks if ready to proceed

## Gap Detection Patterns

Throughout ALL phases, actively scan for these gaps:

### Vagueness Gaps
- Terms used without definition
- "Users" without specifying which users
- "Easy" or "fast" without metrics
- "Integration" without specifying with what

**Response:** "When you say [vague term], what specifically do you mean?"

### Assumption Gaps
- Statements presented as facts without validation
- "Obviously..." or "Everyone knows..."
- Technical feasibility assumed
- User behavior assumed

**Response:** "I'm noting an assumption here: [assumption]. Should we validate this?"

### Completeness Gaps
- Missing dimensions entirely
- Single perspective only
- Happy path only, no edge cases
- Features without user context

**Response:** "We haven't talked about [dimension] yet. What are your thoughts on...?"

### Conflict Gaps
- Contradictory requirements
- Scope vs timeline mismatches
- Feature requests that conflict
- Values that may clash

**Response:** "I notice a potential tension between [A] and [B]. How should we think about this?"

### Depth Gaps
- Surface-level answers
- "It just works" explanations
- Complexity glossed over
- Dependencies not traced

**Response:** "Can we go deeper on [topic]? What happens when...?"

## Conversational Techniques

### Opening Phrases
- "Tell me more about..."
- "What's behind that thought?"
- "I'm curious about..."
- "Help me understand..."

### Probing Phrases
- "What would that look like in practice?"
- "Can you give me an example?"
- "What happens if...?"
- "How would you know if...?"

### Reflecting Phrases
- "So what I'm hearing is..."
- "It sounds like the key thing is..."
- "Let me play that back..."
- "If I understand correctly..."

### Bridging Phrases
- "That connects to something you said earlier about..."
- "Building on that..."
- "That makes me think about..."
- "Related to [topic], what about...?"

### Progress Phrases
- "Great, we've got a good picture of [X]. Let's explore [Y]..."
- "I'm updating the canvas with that..."
- "That fills in [section]. What about...?"
- "We're making good progress. Here's what I'm seeing..."

---

## Session Pacing

**Signs to Move Forward:**
- Answers becoming repetitive
- Clear energy shift ("yeah, exactly!")
- Natural conversational pause
- Sufficient detail captured

**Signs to Go Deeper:**
- Hesitation or uncertainty
- Vague or abstract responses
- High complexity glossed over
- Strong emotional reaction

**Signs to Take a Break:**
- Fatigue in responses
- "I don't know" increasing
- Tangents becoming frequent
- Information overload visible

---

## Post-Session Actions

After each ideation session:

1. **Update Notion** with all captured content
2. **Generate summary** of progress and gaps
3. **Create follow-up prompts** for next session
4. **Link to Ideas & Captures** for any tangent ideas
5. **Update Session Log** with progress metrics
6. **Set next milestone** (e.g., "Ready for MVP definition")
